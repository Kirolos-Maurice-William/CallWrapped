**Date:** September 27, 2026  
**Status:** Disabled in Production (`ACOUSTIC_FUSION_ENABLED=0`); Infrastructure Retained in Shadow Mode  
**Scope:** Feature B (Sessions 3–4) — `bot/audio/loudness.py`, `bot/audio/fusion.py`, `audit/run_fusion_proof.py`

## 1. Executive Summary

Feature B introduced acoustic loudness tracking (`SpeakerLoudnessBaseline`, `UtteranceLoudnessAccumulator`) and deterministic late fusion (`fuse_anger`) to complement text-based anger detection from the batch classifier. 

When evaluated across **53 real captured clips** from three human-annotated dispute sessions (Batch 2: Movies, Batch 3: World Cup, Batch 4: Real Estate), acoustic fusion produced a **measured net regression**:
- **Shouted/Elevated Subset (37 clips):** Text-only agreement = **10.8%** (4/37) vs Fused agreement = **2.7%** (1/37) $\rightarrow$ **Net Lift: -8.1%** (-3 clips).
- **Full 3-Session Corpus (53 clips):** Text-only accuracy = **37.7%** (20/53) vs Fused accuracy = **32.1%** (17/53) $\rightarrow$ **Net Lift: -5.7%**.

In accordance with strict empirical discipline, **acoustic fusion is disabled by default in production** via `ACOUSTIC_FUSION_ENABLED=0`. All underlying acoustic processing, feature extraction, and shadow logging (`audit/shadow/loudness_shadow.jsonl`) remain active in shadow mode for continuous dataset expansion.

## 2. Hypothesis & Architectural Design

### Original Hypothesis
Large Language Models running batch classification over ASR transcripts suffer false negatives when Egyptian conversational shouting or escalation occurs without overt vulgarity or explicit lexical insult cues (e.g. arguing about dates, plot details, or price numbers with intense vocal energy). 

The hypothesis posited that combining log-RMS acoustic energy with textual anger probabilities would recover these conversational shouting misses by elevating sub-threshold utterances ($p_{\text{text}} \approx 0.0$ or $0.5$) to mild or high anger when sustained volume spikes occurred ($z_{\text{robust}} > 2.5$).

### Architecture Built
1. **Per-Speaker Baseline (`SpeakerLoudnessBaseline`):**
   - Sliding window of 1200 frames (24 seconds) of voiced, unclipped 20ms frames in log-RMS dB space.
   - Robust median and Median Absolute Deviation ($\text{MAD} \times 1.4826$).
   - Strict update gate: frames flagged as loud ($z > 2.0$) or clipped are excluded from updating the baseline.
2. **Utterance Accumulator (`UtteranceLoudnessAccumulator`):**
   - Streamed 20ms frames per active voice speaker.
   - Computes $p_{95}$ dB level, peak robust $z$-score, sustained spike count ($\ge 3$ consecutive frames with $z \ge 2.5$), and clipping ratio.
3. **Deterministic Late Fusion (`fuse_anger`):**
   - $p_{\text{text}} \in \{0.0, 0.5, 0.9\}$ for none, mild, high.
   - $\text{boost} = 0.6 \times \text{clamp}\left(\frac{\text{peak\_z} - 2.5}{2.5}, 0, 1\right)$ when `was_loud`, `calibrated`, and `clip_ratio < 0.05`.
   - $p_{\text{fused}} = 1 - (1 - p_{\text{text}})(1 - \text{boost})$.
   - Final classification: $p_{\text{fused}} \ge 0.8 \rightarrow \text{high}$, $\ge 0.45 \rightarrow \text{mild}$, else $\text{none}$.
   - Hard excitement guard: suppresses boost if neither frustration cues nor an active dispute is present.

## 4. Empirical Root Cause Analysis

Three distinct, measurable phenomena explain why loudness energy alone failed to deliver positive lift:

### Root Cause 1: Acoustic Magnitude Inversion (Banter vs Anger)
Loudness magnitude does not cleanly separate anger from conversational banter or humorous laughter:
- In the **Batch 2 Movies dispute**, playful banter and laughter by speaker `Drago` reached massive relative $z$-scores ($z = 14.98$, $18.92$, and $24.24$). Because an active dispute was simultaneously registered on the thread, the excitement guard did not prevent boosting, misclassifying these banter turns as `mild` anger (3 regressions).
- In contrast, in the **Batch 4 Real Estate dispute**, genuinely angry, high-frustration utterances by `2xDanger` only reached peak $z$-scores of $2.90$ and $3.14$.

### Root Cause 2: Unreachable Boost Slope for Cold Utterances
When text classification returns `none` ($p_{\text{text}} = 0.0$), the mathematical formula requires:
$$p_{\text{fused}} = \text{boost} \ge 0.45$$
Solving for $z$:
$$0.6 \times \frac{z - 2.5}{2.5} \ge 0.45 \iff \frac{z - 2.5}{2.5} \ge 0.75 \iff z \ge 4.375$$
In real-world shouting clips (e.g. `clip_015` and `clip_023` in Batch 4), natural shouting reached $z \in [2.8, 3.5]$. This yielded a boost of only $0.10\text{--}0.15$, leaving $p_{\text{fused}} \le 0.15 \ll 0.45$. The acoustic signal was thus completely incapable of rescuing true conversational misses.

### Root Cause 3: Baseline Normalization Under Group Escalation
When a multi-party dispute escalates (as observed in the World Cup and Real Estate sessions), all participants raise their voices simultaneously over multiple dialogue turns. As a speaker continues to talk loudly, their individual running baseline window absorbs the elevated volume levels. Consequently, relative deviation ($z$-score) collapses back down towards $1.0\text{--}2.2$, hiding severe conversational escalation from per-speaker baselines.

## 5. Architectural Decision & Future Roadmap

### Immediate Decision
1. Set `ACOUSTIC_FUSION_ENABLED=0` in `bot/config.py` and `.env.example`.
2. In `bot/arbitration/engine.py`, bypass `fuse_anger` at inference time:
   $$\text{anger} = \text{raw\_anger}$$
3. Keep `SpeakerLoudnessBaseline`, `UtteranceLoudnessAccumulator`, and `log_loudness_shadow` active in background shadow mode. All telemetry logs to `audit/shadow/loudness_shadow.jsonl`.

### Prerequisites for Viable Acoustic Fusion
To make multi-modal emotion detection effective in Discord voice calls, future work must incorporate:
1. **Prosodic Pitch & F0 Contour Tracking:** Fundamental frequency ($F_0$) trajectory and vocal strain/jitter provide significantly higher discriminative power between laugh-shouting and angry shouting than raw RMS volume alone.
2. **Global Session / Room-Relative Baselines:** Measuring a speaker's volume relative to the ambient channel energy and cross-speaker dynamics, rather than solely their own historical median.
3. **Conversational Turn Context:** Feeding acoustic metadata into an acoustic-aware classification prompt or a lightweight trained fusion model rather than fixed-threshold heuristic mathematical formulas.

### Parked Design Note: Intra-Utterance Baseline Adaptation

> **Status:** Known limitation, parked until acoustic feature returns with pitch.
>
> The per-speaker `SpeakerLoudnessBaseline` updates in real-time during `receiver.py:write()`.
> For long utterances that start quiet and escalate to a shout, sub-threshold frames
> ($z < 2.5$) are absorbed into the running baseline *during* the same utterance,
> before `UtteranceLoudnessAccumulator.finalize()` scores the utterance's peak $z$-score.
> This slightly depresses the computed peak $z$ for gradually escalating speech.
>
> **Revisit when:** acoustic feature returns with pitch/F0 contour extraction — at that
> point, consider freezing the baseline snapshot at utterance-begin and scoring the
> entire utterance against the frozen snapshot rather than the live-updating window.
